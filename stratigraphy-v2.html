STRATIGRAPHY:

7 PRINCIPLES (IMP):

  1. SUPERPOSITION
  2. ORIGINAL HORIZONTALITY:
    1. USING MUD CRACKS: ALWAYS OPEN UP IN UPWARDS DIRECTION
    2. SIZE DECREASE OF GRAINS: SIZE USUALLY DECREASES UPWARDS
  3. ORIGINAL LATERAL CONTINUITY:
    1. PINCHING OUT
    2. LAYERING AGAINST UNEVEN ROCKS
    3. EROSION
    4. CUT BY A FAULT
  4. UNIFORMITARIANISM
  5. BIOLOGICAL SUCCESSION (ALSO KNOWN AS LAW OF FAUNAL SUCCESSION)
  6. CROSS CUTTING
  7. INCLUSIONS

STRATIGRAPHIC BORDERS:

  1. INTRUSIVE:
    1. SILLS (CONCORDANT)
    2. DYKES (DISCORDANT)
  2. DEPOSITIONAL CONTACT:
    1. VERTICAL:
      1. CONFORMABLE
      2. UNCONFORMABLE
    2. LATERAL:
      1. ABRUPT
      2. GRADATIONAL
      3. INTERTONGUING
      4. WEDGE

UNCONFORMITIES (IMP):

  1. ANGULAR: FORMS HORIZONTAL, NATURAL FORCES BEND, TRUNCATION BY EROSION, NEW LAYER ON TOP
  2. DISCONFORMITY: BEDS ABOVE AND BELOW ARE PARALLEL, BUT THERE IS A MISSING CHUNK OF TIME, EROSIONAL DIFFERENCE IS VISIBLE IN A DISCONFORMITY
  3. PARACONFORMITY: SAME AS A DISCONFORMITY, BUT TIME GAP IS NOT AS EFFECTIVELY VISIBLE, INSTEAD WE USE BIOLOGICAL DEPOSITS TO MEASURE DIFFERENCE IN TIME
  4. NONCONFORMITY: SEDIMENTARY ROCKS ARE DEPOSITED OVER IGNEOUS OR METAMORPHIC BASES

STRATIGRAPHIC CLASSIFICATIONS:

  1. LITHOSTRATIGRAPHIC UNITS: ON THE BASIS OF PHYSICAL PROPERTIES
  2. BIOSTRATIGRAPHIC: USING BIOLOGICAL DEPOSITION CONTEXTS SUCH AS FOSSILS
    1. FAD: FIRST APPEARANCE DATUM - BEGINNING BOTTOM LAYER
    2. LAD: LAST APPEARANCE DATUM - LAYER OF EXTINCTION

    TYPES OF BIOZONES:

    1. INTERVAL ZONE: INTERSECTION OF TWO GROUPS A AND B
    2. ABUNDANCE ZONE: ZONE WHERE ONE GROUP IS PRESENT IN HIGH AMOUNTS
    3. ASSEMBLAGE ZONE: ZONE WHERE GROUPS A, B AND C ARE PRESENT
  3. UNCONFORMITY BOUNDED UNITS
  4. MAGNETOSTRATIGRAPHIC UNITS: STRIPES OF OPPOSING POLARISATION IN ROCKS DUE TO PERMANENT MAGNETISATION DUE TO REVERSAL OF MAGNETIC FIELD OF THE EARTH. ITS CHRONS: BRUNHES, MATUYAMA, GAUSS, GILBERT (FROM YOUNGER TO OLDER ORDER)
  5. CHRONOSTRATIGRAPHIC UNITS

TIME SCALE:

  1. GEOCHRONOLOGICAL TIME SCALE: A SYSTEM OF HIERARCHICAL TIME SCALES USED FOR MEASURING GEOLOGICAL TIME
    GEOCHRONOLOGICAL COLUMN: THE COLUMN OF STRATA CORRESPONDING FOR THAT TIME SCALE

    4. PHANEROZOIC
    (ORDER IS OLDEST TO YOUNGEST)
  3. PHANEROZOIC (OLDEST TO YOUNGEST):
    1. PALEOZOIC: Cambrian → Ordovician → Silurian → Devonian → Carboniferous → Permian
    2. MESOZOIC: Triassic → Jurassic → Cretaceous
    3. CENOZOIC: Paleogene → Neogene → Quaternary
  4. EXTRAS:

    Arthur Holmes constructed an early geological time scale by compiling the cumulative thicknesses of sedimentary successions from different parts of the world, particularly through fossils and biological deposits.

    Good index fossils should ideally be:

    • Geographically widespread.
    • Easily identifiable.
    • Abundant.
    • Restricted to a relatively short geological time interval.

    $$\text{Sedimentation rate} = \frac{\text{Thickness of sediment}}{\text{Time represented}}$$
